# Copy rules and bank

Used by every build, and by `/copy`.

## Formulas for hero headlines (pick one, then cut words)

- What it is, for whom: "Climbing for every level."
- What it does: "Fresh roasted, shipped this week."
- A plain claim the business can prove: "Built for the open road."

Maximum 8 words, two lines. If the headline needs the paragraph to make sense, rewrite the headline.

## Supporting sentence

One sentence, under 20 words. It adds one fact the headline did not: what you offer, where, or how. It never repeats the headline in softer words.

## Buttons

Verb plus object, or a destination. Primary is the action most visitors want; secondary is the second most common action.

Good: Shop coffee, Book a day pass, Visit our showroom, See our walls, Call the shop, Get directions.
Bad: Get started, Learn more, Discover, Explore now, Click here, Submit.

## Rewrites (bad to good)

| Bad | Good |
|---|---|
| Elevate your riding experience | Motorcycles built for the open road |
| Where craftsmanship meets passion | Handmade furniture, built to order |
| Unleash your inner athlete | Strength and cardio classes, six days a week |
| Seamless solutions for modern teams | Project tracking for small teams |
| Your journey starts here | Book your first lesson |

## Banned words and phrases

elevate, unleash, reimagine, seamless, empower, journey, next-generation, cutting-edge, game-changer, revolutionize, unlock the, "where X meets Y", "crafted for the modern X", "experience the difference", "more than just a X", "learn more", "get started".

## Invented content and placeholders

- Never invent statistics, reviews, testimonials, awards, logos, prices, opening hours, or addresses. If a section needs them, ask the user for the real ones.
- If the user wants to see the layout before supplying facts, write short realistic sample content (product names, one-line descriptions), and tell the user in your reply which items are samples. Do not write "Sample copy", "Lorem ipsum", "Coming soon", or bracket tags in the page itself.
- Product names and descriptions invented for a demo are fine to include when the user asked for a demo; say so in one line.
